A chatbot demo takes an afternoon. An assistant that people trust with real work takes something else: access to the right data, the right permissions, and a clear line between what the model drafts and what a person decides.

## Where AI earns its place

In our AI transformation work, the systems that last tend to fall into four groups.

- Production assistants that answer questions from your own documents, policies, and records.
- Automation for repetitive steps — classifying, routing, summarizing, and filling forms.
- Document intelligence that reads contracts, applications, and reports and pulls out what matters.
- Decision support that brings the relevant data together so a person can decide faster.

## Connect before you generate

The model is rarely the hard part. The hard part is reaching the data safely: which systems hold it, who is allowed to see it, how fresh it must be, and how to log what the assistant used. We design that layer first, inside your existing stack, so the AI works on the same truth your teams work on.

## Keep people on the decision

Every flow we deliver marks where the agent drafts and where a person approves. That is not a limitation; it is what lets an institution put AI into a regulated process without giving up accountability.

> An assistant is only as useful as the data it is allowed to read and the review it has to pass.

## A practical first step

Pick one process where people spend hours reading or re-typing information. Bring an example of the documents involved and who signs off at the end. We can map the data, show where AI fits, and plan a first flow that runs in production — then extend from there.
