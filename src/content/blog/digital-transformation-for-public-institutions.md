In Turkey, MasterFabric is a stakeholder in the digital transformation of public institutions, with work concentrated in education and military. These are environments where software is not a side project. It runs how people teach, train, and operate every day.

## What makes public-sector work different

- The process is often defined by regulation, not by preference, so the software must follow it exactly.
- Security, data residency, and access control are requirements from the first day, not features added later.
- Users range from specialists to people who touch the system once a month; both must succeed.
- The institution needs to own and operate the result long after the project team leaves.

## How we approach it

We start with the process the institution already runs and write the system around it. Architecture, interfaces, and where AI belongs are decided before production code is written. Agents can draft and test, but engineers stay on design, security, and every call that should not be automated.

## Education and training

For education and training, that usually means systems that manage people, content, and progress in one place, with reporting the institution can trust. AI can help prepare material or summarize results, while instructors stay in control of what is taught and assessed.

## Built to be handed over

Public institutions cannot depend on a single vendor forever. Every delivery ends with documentation, training, and software the institution owns — so the next change does not start from zero.

> In public work, the measure of success is a system people rely on and an institution that can run it.
