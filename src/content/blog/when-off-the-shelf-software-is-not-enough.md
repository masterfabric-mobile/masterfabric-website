Most organizations start with a packaged product, and many should. A good SaaS tool is cheaper to start, quicker to roll out, and someone else maintains it. The trouble begins when the process the business actually runs no longer fits the shape of the tool.

## Signs you have outgrown the package

- Your team keeps a spreadsheet next to the tool because the tool cannot hold what they need.
- Every new requirement becomes a workaround, a plugin, or a request the vendor will not prioritize.
- Data you own is locked in a format you cannot query, move, or connect to the rest of your stack.
- The licence grows with every seat, but the value does not.
- An AI feature you want depends on data the tool will not expose.

## What bespoke software changes

A bespoke system is written for one organization. It follows the process you already run instead of asking people to bend around a product roadmap. The data model is yours, the integrations are the ones you need, and the software is owned by you when the project ends — not rented.

## When we tell you not to build

MasterFabric does not sell a one-size-fits-all platform. That also means we have no reason to push a build when a ready-made tool already solves the problem. In the first conversation we look at what exists. If a product fits, we say so. If it does not, we plan the system that was asked for.

> The right answer is the one that fits your process, not the one that fits our backlog.
